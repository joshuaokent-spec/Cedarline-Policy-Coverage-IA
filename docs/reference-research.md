# Reference Research

This project is a fictional Cedarline Mutual redesign. It is **not** a reproduction of any insurer's private systems or research.

To ground the IA problem in a realistic insurance-content environment, I reviewed publicly available insurance-site patterns, including Auto-Owners Insurance.

## Public patterns observed

### Product taxonomy spans multiple major lines

Auto-Owners' public Insurance Policies page groups products under broad areas including Home, Auto, Life and Business, with many individual products beneath those groups.

Examples include:

- Homeowners
- Condo
- Renters
- Umbrella
- Flood
- Auto
- Motorcycle
- RV
- Boat
- Life insurance
- Business insurance
- Commercial auto
- Workers compensation

Reference:
https://www.auto-owners.com/insurance

### Existing customers have task-oriented needs separate from shopping

Auto-Owners' Online Access page describes functions such as:

- pay bills / recurring payments;
- view policy documents;
- review coverages and premiums;
- access proof of insurance;
- access current claims and claim history;
- request roadside assistance.

These tasks represent a different mental model from browsing insurance products.

Reference:
https://www.auto-owners.com/about/online-access

### Claim information forms its own service domain

Public claims pages include tasks such as:

- claim status;
- claim representative information;
- claim tracking;
- claim reporting guidance.

Reference:
https://www.auto-owners.com/claims/report-a-claim
https://www.auto-owners.com/claim-tracker

### Coverage pages contain both formal terms and user-facing explanations

The public Homeowners Coverages page includes formal coverage concepts such as:

- Dwelling
- Other Structures
- Personal Property
- Personal Liability
- Medical Payments to Others
- Additional Living Expenses

It also includes optional coverage concepts.

Reference:
https://www.auto-owners.com/insurance/homeowners/coverages

## IA implications

These patterns support several hypotheses for the Cedarline redesign:

1. **Product browsing and policy-management tasks should not compete in the same taxonomy.**
2. **Users may need multiple entry points into coverage information.**
3. **Formal insurance terminology should remain available, but plain-language information scent can help users reach it.**
4. **Claims, billing, account management and product education are distinct user intents.**
5. **A scalable IA needs to support both many product types and high-frequency service tasks.**

## What this research does not prove

This desk research does not establish:

- how real users mentally group these topics;
- which navigation labels perform best;
- tree-test success rates;
- task-completion rates;
- whether the proposed Cedarline structure is superior.

Those questions require primary research and validation. The project therefore includes a separate validation plan rather than presenting the proposed taxonomy as a tested result.
